What am I looking at?

The intersection of a tilted circular cylinder and a horizontal plane is an ellipse. Increasing the tilt stretches its major axis; raising the plane moves its center away from the origin. Global rotation turns the ellipse and its orbit together.

For a cylinder of radius \(r\), tilted by \(\alpha\) from vertical, the semi-axis lengths are:

\[a = \frac{r}{\cos\alpha}, \qquad b = r.\]

At plane height \(h\), the center follows a circle of radius \(h\tan\alpha\):

\[X_c = h\tan\alpha\cos\theta, \qquad Y_c = h\tan\alpha\sin\theta.\]

The dashed circle shows that orbit. When present, the shaded blue disk in the top view has radius \(r-h\tan\alpha\).
